## About the Album

_Sales Already Promised It_ is a glam-metal workplace story about a promise that becomes a
liability. At the fictional software company Infinite Loop Solutions, Sales signs a major
deal before Engineering has seen the requirements. What begins as a familiar handoff turns
into a fight over scope, ownership, and who has to tell TitanBank that the product cannot
do everything in the contract.

The album uses glam metal and blues rock for the right kind of excess. The big guitars and
choruses match the confidence of a pitch meeting; the slower passages carry the pressure
that follows. Its recurring image is the quarterly graph, first treated as a trophy and
later as a warning. The music keeps the story moving without softening the details that
make the problem recognizable.

Cassidy Stone, the sales director, sits at the center of the record. She can command a
room and knows what anxious executives want to hear, but she is also working under a quota
that could cost her team their jobs. The album does not turn her into a simple villain.
Her decisions create the crisis, yet the company’s incentives make those decisions feel
routine until they land on someone else’s desk.

The opening songs set up the sales pitch. “End of the Quarter” introduces the target that
drives every decision, while “Fully Customizable” turns each customer request into another
easy yes. “The Commission Is Calling” gives Cassidy’s confidence a cost: she knows that a
missed number will lead to cuts, even if the deal she needs creates problems for everyone
else.

“Sign Now, Build Later” and “Eight Weeks to Impossible” move the record into the delivery
phase. Leon and the development team discover the deal after it is already binding. The
requirements are too broad, the deadline is too short, and the language of the pitch has
become an obligation. “The Demo Was Mostly Real” lets the polished presentation break down
under ordinary questions. By “TitanBank Wants Everything,” every casual promise has
returned as a formal demand.

The second half lets Engineering answer back. “Engineering Joins the Call” replaces broad
assurances with limits and dependencies, while “That Was Not in the Contract” turns the
same document into an argument among Sales, Engineering, legal counsel, and TitanBank.
“Quota or Conscience” gives Cassidy the central choice: repeat the promise or say what the
company can actually deliver.

The resolution stays modest. In “Tell Them What We Have,” Cassidy presents the real
product with its limits stated plainly. “A Smaller Honest Deal” accepts a narrower
contract and a schedule the team can meet. The last track, “One More Enterprise Request,”
leaves the pressure intact. A new demand arrives, and the company has not suddenly become
a better place to work.

Recommended if you like: glam metal, blues rock, arena-ready hooks, satirical concept
albums, and workplace stories with clear stakes.
