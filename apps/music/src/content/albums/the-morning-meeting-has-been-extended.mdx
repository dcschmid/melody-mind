## About the Album

Current’s newsroom is functioning, confident, and thoroughly accustomed to running on bad
coffee and editorial instinct. The opening track, Just Five Minutes, establishes the
baseline: a crowded calendar, ingrained professional habits, and the introduction of
Audience First as a practical modernization initiative rather than a mandate. When Leo
Brandt installs the real-time dashboard in Put It on the Big Screen, the skeptical editors
watch with the cautious curiosity of people who have seen software promises fail before.
What catches their attention is not the interface, but the immediate utility of the
evidence it provides.

The album’s first major shift arrives with Sara Has the Numbers. Sara Malik discovers that
audience data can validate stories senior colleagues have previously dismissed, using hard
evidence to win support for a digital format that editorial tradition had overlooked. This
is not about abandoning judgment; it is about arming it. The experiment pays off in Green
Arrow Morning, where the first visible success gives the initiative credibility and proves
that the tools Leo installed are doing exactly what they promised.

The turning point comes in The Story We Nearly Buried. A useful local service story, one
that senior editors were prepared to let disappear, is pushed digitally by Sara and Leo.
Its unexpected success forces Adrian Vale to acknowledge that audience information
deserves a formal place in editorial discussion. This is the moment the experiment stops
being an experiment and starts becoming process.

The second half of the album follows the consequences of that decision. Run the Other
Headline demonstrates that controlled testing can improve performance without altering the
journalism itself, making optimization feel sensible rather than compromising. As Blink Is
Already There introduces the competitor’s speed into the editorial conversation, the
newsroom realizes that reaching readers faster is not a betrayal of standards, but a
practical necessity.

Adrian Vale responds by formally adding audience performance to the morning whiteboard in
Put It on the Board. Story meetings acquire an additional question in Before We Move On,
lengthening the discussions and quietly shifting what information receives attention. Ben
Carter notices the familiar shape of another transformation program in Ben Starts
Counting, but admits that this one has produced real benefits.

The album’s ethical center is established in Keep the Judgment, where Adrian and Sara
confront the first clear case where editorial importance and expected performance point in
different directions. They establish a provisional rule: performance data can inform
treatment and priority, but it does not automatically determine whether journalism should
exist. This compromise holds. By The Morning Meeting Has Been Extended, the new full
morning ritual is standard practice, and the compromise works well enough to be permanent.

## Production Notes

The record is defined around dry-to-moderate room hard rock with believable guitar amps
and preserved dynamics. Two electric guitars share the arrangement through tight
twin-guitar interplay, short melodic fills between vocal phrases, and occasional ringing
open chords. The bass is audible and mobile, frequently connecting chord changes rather
than simply doubling the guitars. Punchy acoustic drums with firm snare and lively hi-hat
work provide a forward-moving straight-eighth foundation with human fills and small timing
variations. Vocals sit inside the band rather than floating above it, delivered in a dry,
slightly rough, conversational mid-range with restrained sarcasm in the verses and
stronger open-throated choruses. A short ascending three-note twin-guitar figure
associated with the dashboard appears casually at first and becomes increasingly familiar,
ending the album as ordinary newsroom furniture rather than a fanfare.
